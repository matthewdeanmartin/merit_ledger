# The physician's sons

A skilled physician had many children. While he was away, they drank poison. Some were still in their right minds; others had lost them.

He came home and mixed a good medicine. The ones still in their right minds took it and were cured. The others would not: the poison had turned them against the very thing that would help.

So the physician left the medicine with them and went away, and sent word that he had died. Grief brought the children to their senses. Remembering their father's words, they took the medicine and were well. Then he came home.

The Buddha asks: did the physician lie? No one would say so. In the same way, the Buddha only appears to pass away, so that people stop waiting to be saved and take up the medicine themselves.
